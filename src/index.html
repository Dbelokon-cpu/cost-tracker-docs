Most cost-tracking apps ask people to log time a second time, inside them. Cost Tracker reads Jira's own worklogs directly. It adds the money layer Jira does not have: what each person costs per hour, what a project has spent and earned, and whether it is on budget.

How the pieces fit

A cost project is the app's own entity. It is not a copy of a Jira project: it is defined by a scope (one or more Jira spaces, or any JQL filter), and everything else hangs off it. One cost project can span several Jira projects, and one Jira project can be covered by several cost projects.

  1. Scope decides which issues, and therefore which worklogs, count.
  2. Rates turn each worklog's hours into money. A rate can belong to a person, a role, a cost account or the project, and it can change from a given date.
  3. Expenses add costs that worklogs do not capture, such as licences and contractor invoices.
  4. Revenue (optional) adds what the project earns, so profit and margin appear next to cost.
  5. Budget and milestones (optional) give the cost something to be compared with.
  6. Portfolios and reports combine several projects, or slice one by period, person or account.

Where to open it

Open Cost Tracker from the Apps menu in the Jira sidebar. The app has one global page with three areas: Workspace (Projects, Portfolios, Reports) and Setup (Accounts, Attributes, Roles). It does not add anything to individual Jira projects.
