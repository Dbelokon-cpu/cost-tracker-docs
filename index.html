Cost Tracker

Turns the time your team already logs in Jira into project cost, budget and profit — hourly rates, expenses, revenue and reporting, with no separate time-tracking product to adopt.

Most cost-tracking apps ask you to log time a second time, inside them. Cost Tracker reads Jira's own worklogs directly — nothing to migrate to, nothing else to install, no second place for your team to remember to fill in. It adds the money layer Jira itself doesn't have: who costs what per hour, what a project has spent and earned, and whether it's on budget.

Overview

A cost project is the app's own entity, not a 1:1 wrapper around a Jira project — it's defined by a scope (one or more Jira spaces, or any JQL filter) and everything else hangs off it: rates, roles, expenses, revenue, budget. A cost project can span several Jira projects, so teams that split work across spaces for Jira's own reasons can still see it as one line of cost.

Two ways in: a global page in the Jira Apps sidebar lists every cost project, every portfolio, and every report; or open the app from inside a specific Jira project, and it goes straight to the cost project (or projects) that already cover it, or offers to create one pre-scoped to just that space.

Getting started

  1. Open Cost Tracker from the Jira Apps sidebar, or from inside a Jira project's own sidebar.
  2. Select Create project. Give it a name and a scope — either pick one or more Jira spaces, or write a JQL filter.
  3. Choose the project type. Monetary-based measures cost and budget in a currency you pick, computed from hourly rates. Time-based measures them in hours directly and uses no rates. Choose carefully: this decides whether rates, revenue and billing apply at all.
  4. For a monetary project, set the pricing. Either enter a Default hourly rate under Settings → Rates and revenue, or open the Team tab to set a rate per person or per role.

From there, cost is computed automatically from the time your team already logs on the matching issues. Nothing further is required unless you also want to track expenses, revenue or a budget.

A new monetary project starts with a default rate of 0. Until you set a rate somewhere (default, role, person or cost account), logged hours are counted but cost shows as 0. The app shows a warning banner on Overview, Team and Revenue while this is the case. See Rates.

Scope: spaces or JQLSettings → Scope

A cost project's scope is set once, from one of two modes, and can be changed later from Settings:

ModeWhat it matches
Jira SpacesOne or more Jira projects, OR'd together — pick them from a list.
Jira FilterAny JQL you write — for scopes a space selector can't express, like a label across several projects.

Either way, the scope resolves to one JQL string, validated against your site before it's saved. That string is everything the cost report and worklog scan ever read — a cost project can therefore cover zero, one, or several Jira projects, and a single Jira project can be covered by more than one cost project at once (for example, split by cost account instead of by space).

Rates, roles, teamTeam tab · Settings → Rates and revenue

Applies to monetary projects. A time-based project has no rates: the Team tab shows hours only. Roles still work there and feed the "Top roles" breakdown.

Who appears on the Team tab

Everyone who has logged time in the project's scope appears automatically, plus anyone you add explicitly. A role or a personal rate is optional and never a condition for being counted. You can add people who have not logged anything yet by searching Jira users. Only existing users of your Jira site can be found; the app cannot invite people or send email, so invite a new colleague to the site first.

How an hourly rate is chosen

Every worklog is priced individually. For each one the app takes the first rate it finds in this order, and stops:

PrioritySourceWhere you set it
1 (highest)The person's own rateTeam tab, rate field on the person's row
2The rate of the cost account the issue belongs toAccounts section
3The rate of the person's roleTeam tab, Roles
4 (fallback)The project's Default hourly rateSettings → Rates and revenue

Because an account's rate depends on the issue and not on the person, the same person can be priced differently on different issues in the same period. The Team tab labels each rate by where it came from: their own rate, account, role, default rate, or mixed when an account's rate applied to only some of their worklogs. For a "mixed" person the rate shown is the average, cost divided by hours.

Example

Anna has a personal rate of 50. Ben has none and belongs to the role Developer (rate 40). The project default is 30. Ben logs 5 hours on an issue that falls into a cost account with a rate of 60, and 5 hours on an ordinary issue.

WorklogRate usedWhyCost
Anna, 10 h, any issue50Her own rate beats everything, including the account's 60500
Ben, 5 h, issue in the account60No personal rate, so the account's rate wins over his role300
Ben, 5 h, ordinary issue40No personal or account rate, so his role applies200
Carol (no role), 4 h30Nothing else matches, so the project default applies120

Cost rate and billing rate

Each person, role and account can carry two rates: a cost rate (what the person costs the business) and a billing rate (what the client is charged). They are resolved independently with the same four-step order, so they can come from different tiers. Billing rates only matter when hourly revenue is on. With a fixed-price project they are hidden.

Empty is not zero

Leaving a rate field empty means "not set here, use the next tier". Entering 0 means "this work is free" (unpaid or internal work) and is respected as a real rate. Clearing a field you filled in earlier removes the value completely and returns the person to the next tier; it does not leave a hidden zero.

Roles

A role is a named group with a shared cost and billing rate, for example Developer or Designer. Roles belong to one cost project and are not shared between projects. Deleting a role does not remove its people; they are simply unassigned and fall to the next tier. Roles are limited to 60 characters and their names must be unique within the project, ignoring case.

Rates that change over time

A person's own rate can have a start date. On the Team tab, open the row's ··· menu and choose Edit cost rate and history (or billing rate). Enter the new rate and, optionally, Effective from:

  • With a date, hours logged on or after that day use the new rate and earlier hours keep their old one. Work logged before the person's first dated rate falls through to the next tier (cost account, role, project default).
  • Without a date, the rate applies to all hours, past and future, and replaces the history. This is how rates behaved before dates were added, and it is the default.
  • Each period records who set it and when. The history list in the same window shows every period.
  • An empty rate with a date starts a period with no own rate, so the person falls through to the next tier from that day.

The Team table shows the rate in force today, for example 55 · 2 periods. In reports a person with more than one rate in the period shows the average (cost divided by hours), labelled mixed where relevant.

Role rates, cost-account rates and the project default do not have dates yet. Changing one of them still applies the new value to all hours, past and future, and recalculates history at the next refresh. To protect closed periods, give people their own dated rates instead of changing a role or default rate, or keep a closed period in a separate cost project.

Rates are stored per project and expressed in that project's currency. Cost accounts are shared across the site, so an account's rate is a plain number applied as-is in each project's own currency, with no conversion.

Expenses

One-time or recurring costs that worklogs don't cover — software licences, contractor invoices, hardware. A recurring expense repeats on an interval (every N months) until the project's end date, a specific end date, or a number of occurrences, and projects forward into the budget forecast the same way actual expenses count backward into it.

Revenue & profitRevenue tab · Settings → Rates and revenue

Available on monetary projects only; a time-based project has no currency to bill in. Turn it on per project, in one of two modes:

Hourly billing

Revenue accrues from logged hours at each person's billing rate — the same resolution chain as cost, with its own rate.

Fixed price

The project's budget is the contract amount and counts as revenue, regardless of hours logged. Fixed revenue entries (a milestone payment, a licence fee) can be added by hand on top. Billing rates are hidden.

Profit and margin are computed as revenue minus total cost (labour plus expenses) and shown alongside the cost figures throughout, not as a separate reconciliation step.

Budget & milestonesSettings → Rates and revenue · Overview

An optional budget figure, with an optional milestone plan (a schedule of planned spend over time) to compare actual cost against as the project runs. The Overview tab shows schedule, scope and cost progress together, so a budget overrun shows up against the same timeline that's driving it, not just as a single red number.

Portfolios

A portfolio groups several cost projects for a combined view: total cost, total budget, team size and project count, a Gantt-style timeline across all of them, and a table of the member projects with their own status and dates. Deleting a cost project that belongs to a portfolio doesn't break the portfolio — it loads normally with a note that the missing member is excluded from the totals. Hours always add up. Money adds up only across monetary projects that share one currency; with mixed currencies, or only time-based members, money totals are left out and the page says so rather than adding unlike units.

Cost accounts

A named bucket of work — typically CAPEX vs. OPEX, or a client/cost-centre split — defined by a JQL filter rather than a custom Jira field. Work matching an account's JQL (searched inside each project's own scope) can carry its own cost and billing rate, which beats a role's rate and the project default, but not a person's own rate. An issue that matches more than one account's JQL goes to whichever account was created first, so every worklog has exactly one rate. The JQL is checked when you save it; if it later stops parsing, the account is skipped rather than breaking the report. You can create up to 20 accounts, because each one costs an extra Jira search per project. An account's status is informational and only affects filtering in the list; it does not switch the account off.

Reports

A report covers one project or portfolio over a timeframe, broken down by week or month, by person, or by cost account, with totals for hours, labour cost, expenses and total cost. Save a report under a name to re-run it later from the Reports list, edit its settings at any point, or export its current result to XLSX.

Sharing & ownershipSettings → Sharing

Each cost project sets its own general access — everyone using the app (view-only by default) or restricted to a named list — plus individual people added as viewer or editor. Only the project's owner can change sharing, and ownership itself can be handed to someone else, who takes over full control while the previous owner keeps editor access rather than being locked out.

Portfolios are simpler: private to their owner by default, or shared with named people as read-only viewers — no public/everyone option, and no editor tier.

Cost accounts and custom attributes are site-wide setup resources, not scoped to one project, so changing them is limited to Jira site administrators; everyone can still see them.

Jira is only ever read, never written to. Requests to Jira run as the person using the app. Cost Tracker declares no external network access. Details are in the privacy policy and security policy.

Scope limits & caching

A cost project's scope is read up to 500 issues. This is a deliberate limit, sized for small and medium teams. When a project is capped, Overview, Scope and Reports say so explicitly and show the real size ("first 500 of about N issues") instead of silently presenting a partial total as complete. To stay under the limit, split a large project by Jira space or by period; the warning suggests both. If a portfolio has several members, the warning names the project that hit the cap. Cost accounts are limited to 20. Computed reports are cached for roughly 15 minutes and recomputed automatically after that, or immediately after a rates, roles, or settings change that would affect the number.

FAQ

Does it replace Jira's own time tracking?
No. It reads the worklogs your team already logs in Jira. There's no second place to log time.
A project shows zero cost. Why?
Cost comes only from worklogs on issues matching the project's scope. Check the scope JQL in Settings, that the matching issues actually have logged time, and that a rate is actually set. A new monetary project has a default rate of 0 until you set one (see Rates).
I changed a rate and the old numbers changed too. Is that a bug?
It depends on which rate. A person's own rate can have an Effective from date, so older hours keep their old rate; leave the date empty and the change applies to all hours. Role rates, cost-account rates and the project default have no dates yet, so changing them recalculates history. See Rates.
Why does a person show a rate I never entered?
It comes from a lower tier: a cost account, their role, or the project default. The label next to the rate says which. "Mixed" means an account's rate covered only some of their worklogs, and the figure shown is the average.
Time-based or monetary: can I change it later?
Yes, in Settings, and the app asks you to confirm first. Nothing stored is converted: the budget and milestone numbers keep their values and are simply read as hours or as currency amounts afterwards, so review them after switching. Rates, revenue and billing are kept when you go to time-based, only hidden. Changing just the currency changes only the label, so budget, expenses, fixed revenue, milestones and rates are not recalculated.
Why does the app say hourly rates are not set?
A banner on Overview, Team and Revenue appears when logged time is counted at 0 only because no rate applies to it. A new monetary project starts with a default rate of 0. Set a Default hourly rate in Settings → Rates and revenue, or a rate per person or role. A rate you set to 0 on purpose (unpaid work) does not trigger the banner.
A report says the scope was capped. What now?
Only the first 500 issues were read, so totals may be incomplete. The warning shows how many issues the scope really holds ("first 500 of about 1,234"). Split the project by Jira space, or narrow its JQL by period (for example add AND created >= "2026-01-01"), so each cost project stays under the limit.
Can one Jira project belong to more than one cost project?
Yes. Scope is defined independently each time, so the same Jira project can be covered by several cost projects — split by cost account, for instance — at once.
I can't open or edit a project.
Access is per project: owner, editor, viewer, or fully restricted. Ask the project's owner, or a Jira site administrator, to change its sharing.
Where does the data live?
In Forge storage on your own Atlassian site. See the privacy policy for exactly what's kept.
